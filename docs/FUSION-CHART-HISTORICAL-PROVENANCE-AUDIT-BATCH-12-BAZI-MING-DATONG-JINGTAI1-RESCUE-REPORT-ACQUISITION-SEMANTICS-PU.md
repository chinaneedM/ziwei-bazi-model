## 景泰元年月食：救护“报初食”不是观测方法，推算与临时测候必须分层

Status: **FORECAST-vs-LIVE-CEHOU SEPARATION SOURCE-CLOSED / REPORT≠DIRECT-OBSERVATION FIREWALL STRENGTHENED / 1450 CORRECTED-TIME ACQUISITION STILL OPEN / MD-G03 OPEN**

12PT closed the operational trigger: the Ming rescue ritual begins at initial eclipse onset. 12PU asks the next, independent question: when Qintianjian “reports” that trigger, does the report itself prove direct visual detection or a particular physical clock?

The answer is **no**.

The Wanli `《大明會典》` transmission of the dated Hongwu rules says that the Hongwu-26 ritual begins when the Qintianjian officer reports `日初食`, while the Hongwu-6 rule waives the rite under `雨雪雲翳`. This makes weather/visibility operationally relevant to whether the ritual is performed, but it does not state how the officer obtained the report time.

A much stronger near-contemporary control appears in the 1464 `《憲宗實錄》`. Qintianjian officials first submitted one predicted `初虧` time, Tianwensheng Jia Xin submitted another, and the emperor separately ordered `臨時測候`. The record therefore distinguishes **pre-event computed/reported phase times** from a separately named **live observation/verification procedure**.

The later Ming Huidian institutional rule already closed in 12PO supplies a compatible positive control: eclipse phase times and start/recovery geometry are prepared in advance, the bureau officer reports the timing during rescue, and cloudy invisibility does not make reporting intrinsically visual. Zhengde Huidian additionally shows that observation and leak-clock functions coexist institutionally; coexistence does not identify which one produced any particular event time.

Applied to 1450, the Shilu physically says Qintianjian officials `以為辰初初刻` and were impeached for `推測不明`. The `辰初初刻` label is therefore the failed predictive determination. What remains missing is the provenance of the corrected `卯正三刻`: who established it, by what observation/clock/instrument, under what time standard, and at what locality.
