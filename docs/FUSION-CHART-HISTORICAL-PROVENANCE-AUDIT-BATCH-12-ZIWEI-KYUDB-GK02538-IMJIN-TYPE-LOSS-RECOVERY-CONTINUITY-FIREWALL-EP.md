## 1. Why this batch

12EN showed that the dated 1580 and 1604 Inryeokja controls already differ visibly across the shared glyph set. 12EO then tightened the 1580 physical-size control.

The next question is whether 1580 and 1604 may nevertheless be assumed to come from one uninterrupted physical type stock.

They may not.

## 2. Contemporary wartime account

Shen Shou-gyeong's `遣閑雜錄` records the calendar-production sequence around the Imjin invasion:

- before the invasion, calendars were normally printed with cast type;
- in 1592 the capital fell and the calendar apparatus was dispersed/lost;
- officials then produced the Gyeyu calendar by carved blocks;
- after the court returned in winter 1593, someone recovered calendar casting types and presented them;
- calendar printing was then resumed according to former practice.

This public primary-text transcription is not treated as a facsimile. Its substance is independently repeated by the Academy of Korean Studies and the National Science Museum.

## 4. Surviving Ryu calendar media sequence

The official detailed record for the Ryu Seong-ryong series identifies:

```text
1594 甲午   woodblock
1596 丙申   metal Inryeokja
1597 丁酉   metal Inryeokja
1604 甲辰   metal Inryeokja
1606 丙午   metal Inryeokja
```

This is a surviving-object sequence, not a claim that 1596 was the first metal calendar printed after the war.

A generic KOSTMA printing-history table groups 1594–1598 calendars under iron Inryeokja, but the object-detailed heritage record and the Kim Jong-tae 2002 control are more specific for the 1594 Ryu volume. The detailed object record therefore controls: 1594 is woodblock.

## 5. Consequence for the 1580 ↔ 1604 comparison

Because wartime dispersal and later partial recovery are both attested:

- the 1604 stock cannot be presumed to be the complete unchanged 1580 stock;
- the 1604 stock also cannot be presumed wholly new;
- shared/different glyph forms cannot by themselves resolve recovered-vs-replacement sort composition;
- the visible 1580/1604 differences from 12EN remain descriptive, not a standalone casting-generation operator.
